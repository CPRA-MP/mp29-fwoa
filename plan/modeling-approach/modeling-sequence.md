# Modeling Sequence and Starting Conditions

A model’s starting conditions influence its results. The following sections distinguish the observed existing conditions landscape from the modeled landscape used to begin future simulations and explain their role in project comparisons.

Technical experts spent much of 2025 assembling, processing, and preparing survey and satellite data for use in the models. For technical details about the existing conditions data sources and processing methods, see \[LINKS\].

## Existing Conditions {/* #existing-conditions */}

The existing conditions landscape represents the best available empirical description of coastal Louisiana through the end of 2024. CPRA updates this landscape for each master plan. The MP29 update accounts for changes since the landscape data were prepared in 2018 for the 2023 Coastal Master Plan, including land loss and gain, elevation change, land use, vegetation and other ecosystem changes, storm and flood effects, and construction of restoration and risk-reduction projects.

The dataset includes the starting distribution of land and water, vegetation species, habitat types, elevation, bathymetry, hydrologic connections, and other environmental characteristics used in master plan modeling.

## Initial Conditions {/* #initial-conditions */}

Initial conditions represent the modeled landscape at the end of 2029, bridging the interval between the observed [existing conditions](#existing-conditions) landscape and future simulations beginning in 2030.

The initial conditions landscape is produced by running the ICM through a five-year spin-up period covering 2025 through 2029. During this period, the model continues environmental trends, applies anticipated boundary conditions, and adds eligible projects to the landscape according to their expected construction schedules. Projects added to the model landscape during spin-up must satisfy the [MP29 criteria for inclusion in initial conditions](#initial-conditions-project-criteria). For a list of these projects, see \[LINK\].

Boundary conditions are external model inputs that vary over time, such as water levels, river flows, and wind speeds. These inputs are developed from historical observations, trends, and scenario-based projections of future conditions. The storm surge and risk assessment models use the ICM-produced initial conditions landscape and associated boundary conditions, along with information about existing structures and infrastructure, to represent coastal flood risk.

## Future Modeled Conditions and FWOA {/* #future-modeled-conditions-and-fwoa */}

CPRA applies future inputs and scenario assumptions to the end-of-2029 initial conditions landscape over the 50-year planning horizon. Annual outputs support comparisons across years, scenarios, geographic areas, and project alternatives. FWOA simulations describe possible conditions in coastal Louisiana from 2030 through 2079 with no further restoration or risk-reduction actions beyond those in initial conditions. CPRA compares these results with Future With Action simulations to evaluate MP29 Candidate Projects.

The FWOA baseline uses historical and recent observations, model assumptions refined over successive plan updates, and future scenarios. It provides a consistent basis for evaluating project effects across locations, project types, and future conditions. Its results remain subject to the assumptions and limitations of the models.

## Initial Conditions Project Criteria {/* #initial-conditions-project-criteria */}

Projects in initial conditions affect later FWOA and Candidate Project results. CPRA uses defined criteria to determine which projects can be represented with sufficient confidence. Projects must have reached at least one of the following milestones in order to be included in spin-up simulations (started in January 2026):

- construction completed
- construction fully funded
- included for implementation in an initiated Natural Resource Damage Assessment (NRDA) Restoration Plan and Environmental Assessment

The January 2026 cutoff determines project eligibility, not the date when a project enters the modeled landscape. Expected construction schedules determine when eligible projects are added during spin-up. These projects are then included in the baseline for project comparisons. Information about subsequent implementation helps CPRA interpret differences between assumed schedules and actual construction.

### Initial Conditions Project Example {/* #initial-conditions-project-example */}

Some projects included in MP29 initial conditions are expected to have large effects on the future landscape after construction. The following example shows how a project from an earlier master plan becomes part of a later plan’s starting landscape.

The River Reintroduction Into Maurepas Swamp project is included in initial conditions. It includes a gated diversion structure in Reserve, Louisiana, and a conveyance channel crossing Highway 61 and Interstate 10. The project will allow up to 2,000 cubic feet per second (cfs) of Mississippi River water to restore natural swamp hydrology and reduce salinity in the Maurepas Basin. Construction has begun and is expected to be complete \[FILL IN\]. The diversion was selected as a mitigation feature for the West Shore Lake Pontchartrain project. This large-scale structural risk-reduction project is under construction and includes more than 18 miles of levees and floodwalls on the east bank in St. James, St. John the Baptist, and St. Charles parishes. Linking these projects addresses wetland impacts near structural protection and will increase the resilience of the West Shore Lake Pontchartrain project. It demonstrates the master plan’s approach to reducing coastal flood risk and restoring coastal habitats.

Benefits from projects in initial conditions are included in the baseline and are not attributed to the 2029 Coastal Master Plan. They reflect Louisiana’s investment in large-scale restoration and remain an important part of the coastal program.
