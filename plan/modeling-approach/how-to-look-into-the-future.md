# How to Look into the Future

## Scenario-Based Project Evaluation

MP29 landscape-change modeling uses five future scenarios to explore uncertainty. Scenario A and Scenario B are used for Candidate Project comparisons and project selection. The [Future Scenarios: Balancing Confidence and Uncertainty](./future-scenarios.md) section describes their [Shared Environmental Inputs (Table 1)](./future-scenarios.md#shared-environmental-inputs) and [Planning Scenario Sea Level Rise assumptions (Table 2)](./future-scenarios.md#planning-scenario-sea-level-rise).

Future simulations under Scenario A and Scenario B will produce a range of possible landscapes and storm-damage conditions associated with different rates of sea level rise under moderate climate assumptions. For planning purposes, this shared climate pathway represents an extension of current trends and policies rather than a future with substantially stronger climate mitigation. It is a scenario assumption, not a prediction that policies or emissions will remain unchanged. Comparing Candidate Project performance across these conditions allows CPRA to evaluate whether project benefits remain consistent or vary as environmental conditions change.

Scenarios show how different future conditions could affect the coast and proposed projects. Evaluating a range of plausible outcomes helps Louisiana prepare and adapt as conditions change. Uncertainty remains in each scenario.

## Model Uncertainty

Model results depend on the assumptions, inputs, processes, and spatial and temporal scales represented. Readers should consider this context when comparing future conditions and project benefits. Exact conditions at a particular place and time remain uncertain.

Uncertainty comes from future environmental conditions, gaps in measurements, modeling assumptions, and model structure. Rates of sea level rise and changes in temperature, precipitation, river flows, and storms remain uncertain. Some coastal processes are easier to observe and represent than others. Models simplify these processes using relationships based on monitoring data, scientific literature, and professional judgment. Interactions among processes add uncertainty. For example, changes in water level may affect salinity, vegetation, land change, habitat, and flood risk in different ways over time.

Model results can support planning when their [uncertainties and limitations](../results/index.md#interpreting-fwoa-results) are understood. Results may respond differently to changes in inputs and assumptions by location, time period, metric, and modeled process. Review, testing against observations, documentation, and updates as new information becomes available help CPRA identify and manage uncertainty.

Model results may change between master plans as observations, starting conditions, future assumptions, and modeling methods are updated. Each plan reflects the data and scientific understanding available during its development. Comparing results between plans requires understanding these changes and their effects. [Testing against observations and technical review](./future-scenarios.md#model-performance-review), along with [scenario comparisons](./future-scenarios.md#interpreting-metrics-across-scenarios), help assess the reliability of each model version and establish confidence in its planning use. Uncertainty about future conditions remains.
