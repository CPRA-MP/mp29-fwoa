# Modeling Framework

The coastal master plan modeling process (Figure 3) begins with establishment of current, or existing conditions, and baseline, or initial conditions, followed by future simulations including Future Without Action (FWOA) and Future With Action (FWA) runs. Future simulations are conducted first with the ICM and then with Risk Models, which incorporate landscape changes predicted by the ICM. Multiple [future scenarios](./future-scenarios.md) produce a range of outcomes for future simulations. Candidate project evaluation involves comparison of FWA to FWOA results from the ICM for restoration projects and Risk Models for risk reduction projects.

## Modeling Sequence

### Existing Conditions

Establishing Existing Conditions requires gathering observed data to create a representation of the current landscape for each plan. Existing conditions for MP29 represent the coastal landscape in 2024 (vs. 2018 for MP23). Updated information incorporated to create existing conditions for MP29 included elevation data from topographic and bathymetric surveys, land-use/land-cover data derived from satellite imagery, vegetation and hydrology observations from the Coastwide Reference Monitoring System (CRMS), and spatial information from CPRA databases (e.g., locations for newly constructed projects and infrastructure). Fully constructed restoration and risk reduction projects are included in Existing Conditions through the update to the Digital Elevation Model (DEM) used for MP29.

### Initial Conditions

Initial conditions capture landscape changes and anticipated project implementation from the existing conditions to the starting point for future simulations, serving as the baseline to assess changes over time. For MP29, initial conditions represent 2029. The initial conditions landscape is the result of an ICM spin-up period from 2025 through 2029. Future simulations begin with the initial conditions landscapes and run from 2030 for 50 years, through 2079.

In addition to projects fully constructed by the end of 2024, which are represented in the existing conditions, restoration and risk reduction projects that are likely to be built by 2029 were added during the ICM spin up period. To be included in the initial conditions landscape, restoration or risk-reduction projects must have reached at least one of the following milestones by January 2026:

- construction completed
- construction fully funded
- included for implementation in an initiated Natural Resource Damage Assessment (NRDA) Restoration Plan and Environmental Assessment

### FWOA

Future Without Action (FWOA) simulations show how Louisiana’s coast could change from initial conditions (2029) over 50 years (through 2079) with no further restoration or risk-reduction actions. The FWOA establishes a range of outcomes across possible future scenarios and provides a basis of comparison for candidate project evaluation.

### FWA and Candidate Project Evaluation {/* #fwa-and-candidate-project-evaluation */}

Candidate restoration and risk-reduction projects are evaluated by comparing FWA outcomes to FWOA outcomes under the same future scenarios to identify modeled project effects.
